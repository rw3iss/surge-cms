## 1. The one-sentence verdict

The site's *head* is well optimised and its *body* is nearly empty — Google is
served a keyword-perfect `<title>` that JavaScript then **overwrites**, and
article pages whose text is **93% invisible** in the initial HTML — while the
brand's real audience (94K Instagram, 32K X) ranks for "surge philly" and the
owned domain does not appear at all.

You do not have an SEO *strategy* problem. You have four mechanical bugs, and
they are all fixable in the codebase.

## 3. P0 — Blockers. Fix these first, in this order.

### 3.1 JavaScript overwrites the optimised title tag ← **biggest single issue**

**Evidence.** Initial HTML vs. rendered DOM on the same URL:

| URL | SSR `<title>` (what you wrote) | Rendered `<title>` (what Google indexes) |
|---|---|---|
| `/` | `Conservative News for Philadelphia & the Tri-State Area \| Surge Media` | **`Surge Media - Home`** |
| `/posts/saveourcities` | `Our Major Cities are in Decay but There is a Fix \| Surge Media` | `Surge Media - Our Major Cities are in Decay but There is a Fix` |

`og:title` is overwritten too. Google renders JavaScript and uses the **rendered**
title, so the homepage's entire keyword-targeted title — the one containing
"Conservative News for Philadelphia" — is being thrown away and replaced with
the word **"Home"**.

**Root cause.** Two separate defects:

1. `packages/cms/src/components/common/seo/SeoHead.tsx` — the `fullTitle` memo
   hard-codes `"{Site Name} - {Page Title}"`. Brand-first wastes the most
   valuable characters in the SERP and makes every result look identical.
2. `packages/cms/src/pages/Home.tsx:43` — passes the literal string
   `title="Home"`. The homepage never reads the site/page `metaTitle` at all.

**Fix.**
- [ ] In `SeoHead.tsx`, change the format to `"{Page Title} | {Site Name}"` to match what SSR already emits, and add a `titleTemplate`/`rawTitle` escape hatch so a page-authored `metaTitle` is used verbatim with no affixing.
- [ ] In `Home.tsx`, read the homepage's `metaTitle` (falling back to the site's) instead of the hardcoded `"Home"`.
- [ ] Add a regression test asserting `SSR <title> === rendered <title>` for `/`, a post, and a CMS page. These two must never diverge again — divergence is invisible in every tool that reads only one of them.

**Verify.** `curl -s https://surgemedia.us/ | grep -o '<title>[^<]*'` must equal
`document.title` after hydration.

### 3.2 Article bodies are not server-rendered — 93% of the text is invisible

**Evidence.** `/posts/saveourcities`: **45 words** in the initial HTML, **628 words**
in the rendered DOM. What a crawler receives is the headline, date, image,
excerpt and tag list — and nothing else. Zero internal links.

**Root cause — confirmed, not inferred.** All three posts have
`content: ""` (0 characters); their text lives entirely in `post_content_blocks`.
`buildPostBody` (`packages/api/src/services/ssr/bodyBuilder.ts:41`) accepts a
`content` string but **has no `blocks` parameter** — unlike `buildPageBody`,
which does walk blocks. `routes.ts:415` therefore passes an empty string.

This is the known gap recorded in `CLAUDE.md`; the audit confirms it is now
load-bearing, because 100% of published articles use blocks.

**Why it matters more than "Google renders JS".** Google does render JS, but
rendering is a deferred, budget-limited second pass. For a news site competing
on freshness that delay is the whole game — and your `NewsArticle` schema
currently asserts an article whose body is absent from the HTML, which is
exactly the inconsistency that suppresses Top Stories eligibility.

**Fix.**
- [ ] Give `buildPostBody` a `blocks` parameter and walk it with `renderBlockForSeo`, mirroring `buildPageBody`.
- [ ] Feed post blocks in at `routes.ts:415` (fetch `post_content_blocks`, assemble with `buildBlockTree`).
- [ ] Assert a minimum: a published post's SSR body must contain >80% of its rendered word count. Fail the build below that.

**Verify.** `curl -s https://surgemedia.us/posts/saveourcities | sed -n '/SSR_BODY_START/,/SSR_BODY_END/p' | wc -w` → expect ~600, currently 45.

### 3.3 Every nonexistent URL returns HTTP 200 with `index, follow`

**Evidence.**

| Probe URL | Status | robots meta | canonical |
|---|---|---|---|
| `/this-page-does-not-exist-xyz123` | **200** | `index, follow` | self-referencing |
| `/posts/no-such-post-xyz` | **200** | `index, follow` | self-referencing |
| `/shop/fake-product-xyz` | **200** | `index, follow` | self-referencing |
| `/random/deep/path` | **200** | `index, follow` | self-referencing |
| `/tags/politics`, `/category/politics`, `/authors` | **200** | `index, follow` | self-referencing |

This is an unbounded soft-404 factory. Any typo'd or scraped link becomes an
indexable, canonical, "please index me" page. It burns crawl budget on a site
that only has 18 real URLs, and site-wide soft-404 volume is a documented
quality signal.

**Fix.**
- [ ] SSR must resolve the route before responding. Unknown page slug / post slug / product → **HTTP 404** with `<meta name="robots" content="noindex">` and **no** canonical.
- [ ] Keep the SPA's pretty 404 design — return it with a 404 status code, not 200.
- [ ] Decide `/tags/*`, `/category/*`, `/authors` deliberately: either build them (§5.2 — they are a real opportunity) or 404 them. Right now they are neither.

**Verify.** `curl -o /dev/null -w "%{http_code}" https://surgemedia.us/nonsense-xyz` → must be `404`.

### 3.4 No author on any article — the biggest E-E-A-T gap

**Evidence.** All three published posts have `author: null` **and**
`authorId: null`. Result: no byline in the HTML, no byline in the rendered page,
and **no `author` property in the `NewsArticle` schema**.

Meanwhile the brand's credibility is entirely personal — Frank Scales and Ian
McGinnis are named in the search results as "fast-rising names in Philadelphia
conservative media", and they appear on your `/team` and `/founders` pages but
are connected to **zero** articles.

For Google News / Top Stories, author attribution is close to a prerequisite.
You are publishing anonymous political commentary, which is the single hardest
category to rank without attribution.

**Fix.**
- [ ] Assign `authorId` on all three existing posts (the Post editor's Author dropdown already exists).
- [ ] Add `author` to the `NewsArticle` JSON-LD in `packages/api/src/services/ssr/schema.ts` — `{"@type":"Person","name":…,"url":"https://surgemedia.us/authors/…"}`.
- [ ] Render a visible byline in `buildPostBody` (the code path already exists — it is skipped because the field is empty).
- [ ] Build author pages at `/authors/:slug` with `Person` schema, photo, bio, credentials, and `sameAs` links to their X/Instagram. Link every byline to them.
- [ ] Make the author a **required** field for publishing, so this cannot regress.

## 4. P1 — High impact, low effort

### 4.1 A draft copy of the homepage is in the sitemap
`/home-draft` — title `Home (Copy)`, 60 words — is **advertised to Google** and
indexable. It is a near-duplicate of `/`.
- [ ] Delete it, or set it to draft, and drop it from the sitemap.
- [ ] Add a sitemap guard: never emit a page whose slug ends in `-draft`/`-copy` or whose status is not `published`.

### 4.2 No http→https and no www→non-www redirect
`http://surgemedia.us/`, `https://www.surgemedia.us/` and
`https://surgemedia.us/` **all return 200 with identical content** — three URLs,
one page. Canonical tags point to the non-www https version, which mitigates it,
but canonicals are a hint and redirects are a directive.
- [ ] 301 `http://*` → `https://` (Cloudflare "Always Use HTTPS").
- [ ] 301 `www.` → apex (Cloudflare redirect rule, or nginx `server_name` block).

### 4.3 Homepage has two `<h1>`s and neither says who you are
Rendered `<h1>`s are a post headline and *"Our Latest Shorts"*. For a brand-name
query the homepage's primary heading should assert the entity.
- [ ] One `<h1>` on `/`, e.g. **"Surge Media — Conservative News for Philadelphia"**. Demote the rest to `<h2>`.
- [ ] Add a short, visible, indexable intro paragraph naming *Surge Media*, *Surge Philly*, *Philadelphia*, and *Pennsylvania*. The homepage currently renders 377 words, almost all of them post headlines.

### 4.4 Title lengths — 10 of 18 pages exceed the SERP limit
| URL | Chars | |
|---|---|---|
| `/posts/khameneis-demise` | **101** | truncated |
| `/posts/shapiros-hypocrisy` | **88** | truncated |
| `/about`, `/donate` | 77 | truncated |
| `/` | 73 | truncated |
| `/newsletter` | 24 | too thin |
| `/home-draft` | 25 | delete (§4.1) |

None of the three posts has a `metaTitle` set — they fall back to the full
headline. Note the `| Surge Media` suffix costs 14 characters on every page.
- [ ] Set `metaTitle` (≤60 chars) on every post and page; lead with the keyword.
- [ ] Rewrite `/newsletter` (24ch title, 19 words of content) and `/hotline` (17 words) — currently near-empty.

### 4.5 Sitemap gaps
- [ ] `/` and `/posts` have **no `lastmod`** — the two URLs that change most often.
- [ ] No `<image:image>` entries.
- [ ] No Google News sitemap (`<news:news>`) — required for the Google News surface, and you are a news publisher.
- [ ] `/shop` returns 200 but is absent from the sitemap.

### 4.6 The sitelinks SearchAction points at a robots-blocked URL
`WebSite` schema declares `target: /search?q={search_term_string}`, but
`robots.txt` contains `Disallow: /search`. Google will not grant a sitelinks
searchbox whose target it cannot crawl.
- [ ] Either allow `/search` (and `noindex` it), or remove the `SearchAction`.

### 4.7 Missing images alt text
3 of 20 images on `/` and 2 of 7 on a post page have empty `alt`. CDN filenames
are opaque (`vesY4z40egD6.webp`) — no keyword value, and no chance in Google Images.
- [ ] Require `alt` on upload. Backfill existing media.
- [ ] Preserve a descriptive slug in the stored filename.

### 4.8 RSS is missing author and full text
The feed declares the `content:encoded` and `dc:creator` namespaces but emits
neither.
- [ ] Add `<dc:creator>` (after §3.4) and `<content:encoded>` with the full article.

## 5. P2 — The actual ranking strategy

Everything above makes the site *eligible*. This section is what wins the two
target queries.

### 5.1 You are losing your own brand SERP to your own social accounts

Live SERP check for **"surge philly"** — the owned domain **does not appear in
the top 9**. What does:

1. `x.com/Surge_Philly` (32K followers)
2. facebook.com
3. Philadelphia Surge (ultimate frisbee — **philadelphiasurge.com**)
4. premierultimateleague.com
5. YouTube (Dawn Stensland interview)
6. `instagram.com/surgephilly` (**94K followers**)
7. Surge Institute Philadelphia
8. philadelphiasurge.com
9. Wikipedia

For **"surge media" philadelphia conservative news**, surgemedia.us ranks ~5th,
behind **GoFundMe**, Wikipedia, and an **Audacy** podcast page.

Read that carefully: you have a 94,000-follower Instagram audience and a
fundraiser page outranking your own website for your own name. The brand has
authority; the domain has not inherited it.

You are also fighting two established homonyms — *Philadelphia Surge* (ultimate
frisbee) and *Surge Media Group* (surgemediagrp.com, a marketing agency) — so
"surge philly" is genuinely contested and disambiguation matters.

**Actions**
- [ ] Add `https://surgemedia.us` as the website link on X, Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, YouTube, GoFundMe, LinkedIn — every profile, consistently. (Your `sameAs` already points outward; nothing points back.)
- [ ] Get the site linked from the Audacy podcast description and any interview write-ups (Dawn Stensland etc.). Earned links from media domains are the fastest authority path here.
- [ ] Claim a **Google Business Profile** (Philadelphia) — local entity confirmation for "surge philly".
- [ ] Create a **Wikidata** entry for Surge Media LLC linking site + socials. Cheap, and it feeds the Knowledge Graph that disambiguates you from the frisbee team.
- [ ] Ensure **NAP consistency** (name, address, phone) across every profile.
- [ ] Add `foundingDate`, `founders` (Person, linked to author pages), `address`, `telephone`, `publishingPrinciples`, `masthead`, `ethicsPolicy` to the `NewsMediaOrganization` schema. These are precisely the E-E-A-T properties Google's news systems read.

### 5.2 Build the tag/section archives you are currently soft-404ing
You already assign rich tags — *Politics, Opinion, Culture, Pennsylvania, Urban,
New York City*. Those are ready-made landing pages.
- [ ] Build `/tags/:slug` with a real H1, intro copy, and the post list. Link them from article pages (which currently have **zero** internal links in SSR).
- [ ] Add them to the sitemap and to the footer.

### 5.3 Content volume is the binding constraint
**Three published articles.** No amount of technical work outranks a competitor
with hundreds. For a news domain, publishing cadence *is* the ranking signal.
- [ ] Target a minimum of 3–5 articles per week.
- [ ] Publish a dedicated, substantial page for each head term — one that genuinely answers *"What is Surge Media?"* and *"What is Surge Philly?"*, since those are the queries you want to own.
- [ ] Repurpose the Instagram/X output that already has 94K reach into indexable articles. That content exists; it is simply not on a surface Google can rank.

### 5.4 Local relevance
- [ ] Use "Philadelphia" and neighbourhood names naturally in headlines and body copy.
- [ ] Cover named local entities (City Council, the DA, specific neighbourhoods) — you already do this well in `/posts/shapiros-hypocrisy`; it is the strongest template you have.

3. **PageSpeed Insights** — a cold field measurement; the site's TTFB is excellent (55 ms) but the homepage loads 86 requests including 18 iframes.
4. **Bing Webmaster Tools** + IndexNow — cheap, and Bing feeds ChatGPT search.
